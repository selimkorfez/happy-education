# Happy Education publishing workflow

This is the day-to-day checklist for publishing an article on the new site. The goal is to make publishing fast without weakening the claims, image-rights or bilingual-content rules built into the website.

## 1. Open the publishing desk

1. Sign in to Sanity Studio at `/studio`.
2. Open **Publishing**.
3. Choose **English articles** or **Türkçe articles**.
4. Create a new **Article** document.

The English and Turkish trees are separate editorial documents. Do not paste a machine translation into the other tree simply to fill it. Create a proper second-language version when it has been reviewed.

## 2. Fill the article essentials

Before writing the body, complete:

- **Title** — specific and useful rather than clickbait.
- **Slug** — concise, lower-case and stable. Changing a published slug later requires a redirect.
- **Excerpt** — one short explanation of what the reader will learn.
- **Author** — a real Happy Education author whose public consent is on file. Never invent an expert byline.
- **Category** and useful tags.
- **Published date**.
- **Reading time** if the article uses it.

## 3. Write for a real student question

A useful article should answer one clear intent such as:

- How do I compare two study destinations?
- What documents should I prepare before applying?
- What should a parent ask a summer-school provider?
- How should I compare language-school course formats?

Use headings that help the reader scan. Prefer short sections, checklists, comparison blocks and FAQs over one long wall of text.

## 4. Verify claims before publishing

Anything that can change needs a current source. Examples include:

- tuition fees and deposits;
- entry requirements;
- visa or immigration rules;
- work rights;
- programme dates and availability;
- rankings;
- scholarships;
- accreditation;
- application deadlines;
- partner or representation claims.

Record the source in the article/review fields where available. If the claim cannot be verified, rewrite it as general guidance or remove it.

Happy Education must not present general visa-process information as regulated immigration advice. Link readers to the relevant government guidance and a regulated adviser when individual immigration advice is needed.

## 5. Add imagery safely

For an uploaded image, complete the media-rights metadata and alt text.

The site deliberately refuses to render CMS photography until `licence.cleared` is true. Do not bypass this gate.

Acceptable sources include:

- Happy Education-owned/commissioned imagery with permission recorded;
- institution media where written reuse permission exists;
- appropriately licensed reusable media such as CC0/Public Domain/CC BY/CC BY-SA, with the creator, source and licence recorded;
- commissioned AI editorial imagery where appropriate and clearly safe to use.

Do not use an image just because it appears in Google Images, on a university website, on Instagram or on the old WordPress media library.

## 6. Complete SEO and related content

Before publishing:

- write a useful SEO title and description where the defaults are not strong enough;
- add related articles and destinations when genuinely relevant;
- add FAQs only when they answer real follow-up questions;
- add one clear CTA rather than repeating consultation buttons after every paragraph;
- confirm the article is in the correct locale.

## 7. Review the preview

Check the draft preview on desktop and mobile before publishing. Confirm:

- there is one clear H1;
- headings are in a sensible order;
- links work;
- external sources open safely;
- no Turkish copy has leaked into an English article or vice versa;
- images have meaningful alt text;
- no uncleared image or unsupported claim is present;
- the page does not contain a large accidental blank area.

## 8. Publish

Publish only after the content and rights checks are complete.

After publishing, open the live article once and verify the canonical URL, image, links and CTA. If a published slug ever changes, create a **Redirect** document immediately.

## Social content

The **Publishing** desk also contains **Social media stories**. These are curated website cards that link to an original Happy Education social post and explain:

1. what the post contains; and
2. why it matters to a student or parent.

The website links out to the original social post rather than loading tracker-heavy third-party embeds by default.

## Student experiences

The **Student experiences** document type has two public gates:

- the quote must be verified as genuine; and
- publication permission must be recorded.

A legacy testimonial being visible on an old website is not treated as proof of current permission. The new site only queries records that pass both gates.
